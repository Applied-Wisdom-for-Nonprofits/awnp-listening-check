2 · Blog Post

You can agree with someone and still make them feel dismissed.

Listen to how you begin your reply. Three words are worth watching: no, but, and however.

We often use them without thinking. We even say, “No, I agree with you.” The intent may be good. The result may not be. The other person hears that you are ready to make your point, not hear theirs.

Marshall and Kelly Goldsmith use a simple exercise. Put aside $20 every time you begin a reply with one of those words. One CEO owed more than $400 before he noticed the habit.

The lesson is not that you must agree. The lesson is to listen first. When someone has a good idea, say, “Good idea.” When you need to understand more, say, “Tell me more.” Then give your view.

Try it for one day at work and at home. You may be surprised by what you hear.
